I’m Welkince, and today I’m really happy to present my final project, **PocketDay**.

Before I explain the technology behind the application, I want to start with a simple question.

**Have you ever checked your bank balance and thought, “I had enough money… so where did it all go?”**

Most of the time, the problem isn't one big expense.
It's the small expenses we make every day — a coffee here, food there, transportation, subscriptions, and many other things that don't feel significant individually.

But by the end of the month, those small expenses can become a much bigger problem.

And that is the idea behind PocketDay.

PocketDay is a **personal finance management application** designed to help users not just record their income and expenses, but actually **understand their financial situation on a daily basis**.

One of the main ideas behind the application is simple:

**Knowing your balance is not the same as knowing how much you can safely spend.**

So PocketDay provides things like daily safe spending, budget tracking, savings goals, subscription tracking, spending analytics, and survival-day calculations.

From a technical perspective, I built the application using **Flutter and Dart**, with **Riverpod for state management** and **Firebase Authentication and Cloud Firestore** for authentication and data storage.

I also designed the application around an **offline-first approach**, so the user experience doesn't have to completely depend on having an active internet connection.

Throughout this project, my focus wasn't only on making the screens look good. I wanted to understand how a real application is structured — how the UI communicates with the state layer, how data moves through repositories, how Firebase handles persistence, and how different parts of the application react when data changes.

So today, I'll take you through the problem behind PocketDay, the solution, its major features, the technologies and architecture I used, how the data flows through the application, some of the challenges I faced, and finally what I learned from building it.

So, let's take a look at **PocketDay**.
